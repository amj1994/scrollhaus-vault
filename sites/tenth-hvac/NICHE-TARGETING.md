# Which Niches Actually Have Budget

2026 benchmark data. Sources at the bottom. CPC and cost-per-lead are used as a
proxy for *proven willingness to spend on customer acquisition* — a business
paying $25 a click has already decided marketing is worth money, which is the
single best predictor that it will pay for a website.

Baseline for comparison: **all-industry average CPC $5.42, CPL $66.69.**

---

## The filter

A niche belongs on the list only if it clears all five:

1. **Proven ad spend** — CPC/CPL well above the $5.42 / $66.69 baseline.
2. **Sells on trust or transformation, not price.** A cinematic story page is
   worthless to a business competing on being cheapest.
3. **Something filmable.** Visible craft, or a visible before/after.
4. **Fragmented.** Thousands of independent operators = thousands of buyers.
   National chains have in-house brand teams and won't buy a template.
5. **Ticket high enough that one extra job pays for the site.** This is the
   entire sales argument.

---

## Tier 1 — build these

| Niche | CPC | Cost / lead | Ticket or patient value |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Personal injury law** | $9.87 | **$131.63** — highest of any vertical | Case values in the tens of thousands |
| **Plastic / cosmetic surgery** | $7.50 (surgical terms $15–60) | $33–150 per consult | ~$6,500 average procedure |
| **Med spa / aesthetics** | $5–15 ("near me" $25–45) | $20–60 non-surgical, $60–180 surgical | **$3,000–10,000 per year, recurring** |
| **Roofing** | **$10.70** (storm/replacement $15–25) | — | Full replacement, high four to five figures |
| **HVAC** ✅ built | $5.31 | $45.27 | LTV **$15,340**; maintenance-plan customers **$47,200** |

**Med spa is the strongest of the five** and the least obvious. It is the only
one with *recurring* annual patient value rather than a one-off ticket, the
market is extremely fragmented, ad costs prove real budget, and the product is
literally a visible transformation. Everything this template does well.

**Personal injury has the highest raw budget of any vertical measured** — but
it's the least filmable in the craft sense. The story has to be the client's,
not the firm's. Doable, different brief.

## Tier 3 — portfolio only, not revenue

Barbershop, salon, café, gym, pet grooming, tattoo.

These produce the most beautiful films and the worst invoices. A barbershop
ticket is roughly $25–40 *(my estimate — I did not find benchmark data for it)*
on thin margins. They are wonderful demos and terrible customers.

**Build the barbershop. Sell the med spa.** The arc I wrote for the barbershop
— someone who stopped recognising themselves, hands doing careful work, the
chair turning to the mirror — is *structurally identical* to a med spa or
cosmetic dentistry story. Same beat, same mirror, same recognition. One has a
$30 ticket; the other is worth $3,000–10,000 a year and spends up to $45 a
click to find customers.

So film it once as your showpiece, then re-shoot the same arc in a treatment
room and sell that version.

---

## Recommendation

**Build 8–10, not 50.**

Fifty templates means fifty video generations, fifty story rewrites, fifty
re-themes — and the long tail is Tier 3, which cannot pay. Concentrate the
production budget on Tier 1 + the top of Tier 2, then widen only if a niche
proves out.

Suggested order:

1. **HVAC** ✅ done
2. **Med spa** — best economics on the list
3. **Roofing** — highest CPC in home services, dramatic footage
4. **Personal injury law** — highest budget anywhere
5. **Cosmetic dentistry** — reuses the med spa arc
6. **Barbershop** — showpiece / lead magnet, priced low or free
7. Emergency plumbing
8. Luxury remodel

---

## Caveat

These are advertising benchmarks, not a market study. They prove a niche
*spends money on acquisition*; they do not prove it buys websites. Before
committing production budget past the first two or three, sell one — a real
invoice from a real med spa is worth more than any of this table.
